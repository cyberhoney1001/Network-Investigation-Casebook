# Scope and Objectives

## Scope

This investigation was limited to an isolated virtual laboratory environment consisting of a Kali Linux machine and an intentionally vulnerable Metasploitable 2 machine.

The investigation focused on the services, authentication mechanisms, software versions, and security weaknesses exposed by the target system.

## In Scope

- Metasploitable 2 target machine
- Services exposed by the target
- Network ports identified during scanning
- Service and version enumeration
- Authentication testing
- Vulnerability research
- Controlled exploitation of identified vulnerabilities
- Privilege verification
- Validation of potential security impact

## Out of Scope

- Public or internet-facing systems
- Third-party networks
- Systems belonging to other individuals or organizations
- Unauthorized access to real accounts or services
- Testing outside the isolated laboratory environment

## Investigation Objective

The primary objective of this investigation is to examine the role of human behavior and security practices in the compromise of a computer system.

Rather than viewing the compromise only as a technical failure, this investigation examines how human decisions, such as weak credential practices,
inadequate system maintenance, and failure to address known security weaknesses, can create or increase opportunities for technical attacks.

The investigation aims to demonstrate that effective cybersecurity is not solely a matter of technical controls.
Human behavior, decision-making, and security practices can significantly influence whether technical vulnerabilities become exploitable attack paths.

## Investigation Questions

This investigation seeks to answer the following questions:

1. What role do humans play in the security of a computer system?

2. Can simple human security weaknesses create opportunities for technical attacks?

3. How can weak credential practices contribute to unauthorized access?

4. How can human decisions around system maintenance and patching contribute to technical vulnerabilities?

5. When a technical vulnerability is exploited, what human or organizational weaknesses may have allowed that vulnerability to remain exploitable?

6. Can a technically secure system still become vulnerable because of human behavior or poor security practices?

7. What can security professionals do to reduce the risk created by human error and decision-making?

## Central Hypothesis

Human behavior and security practices can create conditions that allow relatively simple technical attacks to succeed,
meaning that effective cybersecurity must address both technological vulnerabilities and the humans who operate, maintain, and interact with those systems.
